## 1 &nbsp;&nbsp; Introduction to the SHA Hashing Algorithm

The <b>Secure Hash Algorithms</b> are a family of cryptographic hash functions published by the National Institute of Standards and Technology (NIST) as a U.S. Federal Information Processing Standard (FIPS).<sup>[1]</sup>  
The family of SHA algorithms includes SHA-0, SHA-1, SHA-2, and SHA-3 as follows<sup>[1]</sup>:

<b>SHA-0</b>: refers to the original version of the 160-bit hash function published in 1993 under the name "SHA", which was withdrawn shortly after publication and replaced by version one.

<b>SHA-1</b>: a 160-bit hash function which resembles the earlier MD5 algorithm and was designed by the National Security Agency (NSA) as part of the Digital Signature Algorithm, and which has been deprecated since 2010.

<b>SHA-2</b>: a subfamily of two similar hash functions, with different block sizes, known as SHA-256 and SHA-512, which offer truncated versions of each subfamily and were also designed by the NSA.

<b>SHA-3</b>: a hash function formerly called Keccak, chosen in 2012 after a public competition among non-NSA designers, which supports the same hash lengths as SHA-2 but which has an internal structure that differs significantly from the rest of the SHA family.

The following table outlines the standards that define each of the algorithms within the SHA family<sup>[1]</sup>:

| Standard | Algorithm(s) |
|----------|--------------|
| FIPS PUB 180 | SHA (0) |
| FIPS PUB 180-1 | SHA-1 |
| FIPS PUB 180-2 | SHA-1, SHA-256, SHA-384, SHA-512 |
| FIPS PUB 202 | SHA-3 |
